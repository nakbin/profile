**Abstract**

East Asia has experienced warming faster than the global average, culminating in a record-breaking heatwave during the summer of 2025. Although the three summers from 2023 to 2025 all developed under a persistently negative Pacific Decadal Oscillation, only 2025 produced a heatwave of historic magnitude and persistence over the Korea–Japan region. We show that the meridional structure of the underlying thermal forcing was decisive: in 2023 and 2024, broad tropical–subtropical warming associated with the developing and decaying phases of the 2023/24 El Niño distributed thermal forcing across a wide latitudinal band, whereas in 2025, with the tropics having cooled, warming was confined to the midlatitude North Pacific. This narrow midlatitude-confined forcing strengthened the upper-tropospheric meridional temperature gradient, displaced the East Asian jet poleward, and established persistent blocking, suppressed cloud cover, and enhanced surface shortwave radiation that drove the surface heatwave. A coupled air–sea feedback over the western North Pacific further amplified and sustained the event, and air–sea coupling over this sector has strengthened systematically since 2016, with 2025 representing its most extreme expression on record. These findings indicate that anticipating future East Asian heatwave risk requires understanding the spatial structure of thermal forcing and the evolving strength of regional air–sea coupling, beyond projections of global-mean warming alone.
