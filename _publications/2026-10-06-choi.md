**Abstract**

Boreal-summer circulation exhibits prominent Rossby wave structures over the Northern Hemisphere midlatitudes. These waves can form recurrent circumglobal structures, organize summer circulation, and contribute to persistent and concurrent extreme weather. However, they are commonly identified in seasonal-mean or low-frequency fields, and their characteristics on daily time scales remain less clearly understood. In this study, we examine the characteristics of quasi-stationary Rossby waves (QSRWs) on daily time scales. We first identify dominant stationary Rossby wave patterns from seasonal-mean upper-tropospheric meridional wind, then project daily-mean meridional wind anomalies onto these seasonally defined patterns to measure their day-to-day expression. The leading two patterns are dominated by zonal wavenumbers 6 and 5, respectively. Peak-centered composites show that both patterns remain coherent for about 1 week, with their anomaly centers near the same longitudes throughout the event period. Similar quasi-stationary behavior and persistence are evident in representative individual events. Although the longitudinal positions of the individual centers change little, the phase lines rotate systematically during the event. They are tilted from northwest to southeast before the peak, become nearly meridional around the peak, and tilt from northeast to southwest afterward. This rotation is associated with the poleward increase in phase speed, with the northern portion of each wave center moving relatively eastward and the southern portion moving relatively westward.
