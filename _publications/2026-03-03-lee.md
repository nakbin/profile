**Abstract**

Fire activity in Central Siberia (CS) has increased rapidly in recent decades, yet the subseasonal atmospheric processes driving this interdecadal trend remain poorly understood. We define a daily index representing the amplitude of the long-term fire risk trend pattern, which closely corresponds to observed fire occurrences. Our analysis reveals that enhanced fire activity is primarily modulated by an extratropical Rossby wave pattern resembling the British-Okhotsk Corridor (BOC). This quasi-stationary wave persists for up to two weeks, maintaining anomalous anticyclonic circulation over CS. The associated circulation induces sustained increase in near-surface temperatures, vapor pressure deficit, and latent heat flux, alongside reduced precipitation and soil moisture, thereby creating favorable fire conditions. Fire occurrences typically increase within 2-3 days following anomalous BOC events, indicating a rapid fire response to large-scale forcing. Moreover, fire sensitivity to atmospheric variability has strengthened in the last decade (2010-2020) compared to the previous decade (2001-2010). Consistently, since the mid-2000s, the BOC pattern has increasingly projected onto interdecadal upper-tropospheric circulation trends, accelerating meteorological fire risk beyond background warming.
