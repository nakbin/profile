**Abstract**

As climate prediction systems grow increasingly complex and transition toward higher resolutions, the demand for computational and human resources poses a universal challenge for meteorological centers. In response, South Korea established a customized research-to-operation framework (K-R2O) to consolidate fragmented resources and streamline model innovation. A cornerstone of this framework is the independent APEC Climate Center (APCC) testbed, which serves as a strategic "quality gate" by diagnosing R&D advancements under rigorous, near-operational conditions to ensure their practical readiness before deployment.
This study evaluates the K-R2O process through three diagnostic cases: coupled data assimilation, sea-ice physics adjustment, and a new unified convection scheme. The testbed acted as a validating pathway for the first two advancements, verifying that coupled initialization effectively reduced systematic tropical biases and improved intraseasonal oscillation forecasts, while albedo adjustments enhanced Arctic-midlatitude teleconnections. Both were subsequently integrated into the operational Global Seasonal Forecast System version6 (GloSea6) system. For the new convection scheme, the testbed provided a strategic feedback loop; rather than merely assessing performance, it identified specific areas requiring further optimization—such as water vapor dissipation and convective precipitation—to better align the scheme with operational requirements. By providing objective, evidence-based assessments, the APCC testbed bridges the gap between academic R&D and operational practice, ensuring that technical innovations are not only scientifically sound but also operationally robust.
